## 13. SQL Ownership Test

10 real examples verified directly from the public `sql/` files (not
fabricated):

1. **CASE** (`08_profitability_analysis.sql`) — margin-band
   classification. Technique chosen to bucket continuous margin % into
   readable tiers without a lookup table.
2. **CTE** (`06_customer_analysis.sql`) — `customer_orders`, reused for
   repeat-count, revenue distribution, and top-10 ranking.
3. **Subquery** (`04_product_analysis.sql` family) — products above
   average product revenue.
4. **JOIN** (`09_advanced_analysis.sql`) — fact table to derived
   `product_benchmark`; the one deliberate JOIN, justified because
   Product→Category and Customer→Segment are already 1:1 in the fact
   table.
5. **RANK** (`04_product_analysis.sql`) — product revenue ranking, ties
   share a rank.
6. **DENSE_RANK** (`09_advanced_analysis.sql`) — per-country product
   ranking, no rank gaps after ties.
7. **ROW_NUMBER** — used where a strict unique ordinal per partition is
   needed (top-N customer lists), distinct from RANK's tie-handling.
8. **SUM() OVER** — running/partitioned aggregates in the
   cross-dimensional analysis without collapsing row-level detail.
9. **GROUP BY** (`03_kpi_analysis.sql`, `05_market_analysis.sql`) — core
   aggregation layer for every KPI table.
10. **Aggregation functions** (`SUM`, `COUNT`, `AVG`) — Core/Supporting
    KPI single-row summary.

## 18. Synthetic Data Defense

**"Why did you use synthetic data?"** — To control and fully understand
the ground truth of every relationship in the data, so the analytical
workflow itself — not the specific business story — could be demonstrated
and verified with certainty.

**"Isn't synthetic data a weakness?"** — It limits real-world business
impact claims, which is why I never make any. It does not limit the
validity of the cleaning, KPI, SQL, Excel, or validation methodology,
which is identical to what I'd apply to real data.

**"Why should I care about the project?"** — Because the transferable
skill is the workflow: turning a messy dataset into a validated,
cross-tool-consistent set of business findings — not the specific
(fictional) numbers.

**"What would change with real data?"** — Real customer tenure/
acquisition data (to measure actual retention), real cost decomposition
(to explain margin differences), and a real stakeholder requirements
process instead of self-defined KPIs.

**"Can you claim these business results?"** — No — they describe the
generated dataset as generated, not any real business outcome.
